How to Reduce a PDF to Under 1MB

PBy the PDFNest Team·November 2, 2026·5 min read

A 1MB limit is one of the most common rules on job portals, university applications and government forms, and it catches out scanned documents in particular. Getting under it is usually straightforward once you know what is making the file large. The order matters: identify the cause, fix that, and only then reach for harder compression.

Find out what is making the PDF large

Check the exact size first. On Windows, right-click the file and choose Properties; on a Mac, select it and press Command-I; on a phone, the file app shows it in the details. Then aim for a little under the limit — about 950KB — because portals count megabytes differently and a file at 1.01MB will be rejected.

Next, open the PDF and look at what is on the pages. If the pages are photographs or scans, the file is large because each page is an image, and it will compress well. If the pages are typed text exported from Word or a similar program, the file is probably already compact — and if it is still over 1MB, something specific is inflating it.

Compress scans and image-heavy PDFs

For a scanned document, open Compress PDF, choose Recommended and compress. It rebuilds each page as a well-compressed image, which is exactly what a scan already is, so you lose nothing you had — and a several-megabyte scan often drops well under 1MB. The work happens in your browser; the document is never uploaded.

If Recommended is not enough, compress the original again at Strong. Always start from the original rather than compressing the compressed copy a second time, because each pass softens detail and two passes look worse than one stronger one.

Read the result before uploading. Open it, zoom to 100 percent and check the smallest text — reference numbers, dates, the signature. A file that meets the limit but cannot be read will be rejected by whoever reviews it. If Strong has made text soft, the guide to blurry compressed PDFs covers the alternatives.

Text PDFs need a different approach

A PDF made of typed text is stored very efficiently: the words are text, not pictures of text. Turning those pages into images — which is how page-rebuilding compressors work — can actually make the file larger and stops the text being selectable. When that happens, PDFNest gives you back your original file rather than a bigger one.

If a text PDF is over 1MB, look for the images inside it. A logo saved at poster resolution, a full-size photo or a high-resolution screenshot can account for nearly all of the size. Go back to the source document, replace or shrink those images, and export the PDF again. Most word processors also offer a smaller-size or “minimum size” export option.

If you do not have the source, the content itself may be more than the portal needs. That is the point to remove pages or split the document, covered in the next sections, rather than forcing the text pages through image compression.

Remove pages the portal does not need

Fewer pages beat harsher compression. Scanned bundles often include blank backs of single-sided sheets, duplicate pages, instruction sheets and cover pages that the reviewer does not need. Removing them with Remove PDF Pages reduces the size without touching the quality of what remains.

Read the portal’s instructions for what it actually asks for. “Upload your degree certificate” rarely means the whole 12-page transcript pack, and “proof of address” means one statement page, not the full statement. Sending exactly what is requested is both smaller and easier for the reviewer.

If you need a few specific pages from a longer document, extracting them is faster than deleting everything else. The guide to extracting pages covers choosing the right page numbers.

When one file cannot fit, split it

Some documents are simply too long to be clear under 1MB as a single file. If the portal allows several uploads, split the document into parts with Split PDF and upload each one. Two 600KB files at good quality are better than one 990KB file that nobody can read.

Name the parts so their order is obvious — transcript-part-1-of-2.pdf — because portals do not always keep files in the order you uploaded them. Check that each part makes sense on its own, with no table or section split awkwardly across the boundary.

If the portal accepts only one file and the content genuinely cannot fit, contact the organisation. Many accept an emailed copy or a shared link for oversized documents, and asking is far better than submitting something unreadable. The upload troubleshooting guide covers other options.

Check the file before you upload it

Confirm the final size is under the limit with a little headroom, and that the page count is what you intended. Open the first and last page, and zoom into the smallest text on any scanned page, at the size the reviewer is likely to use.

Rename the file to say what it is and whose it is — for example surname-degree-certificate.pdf. Portals often show reviewers the filename, and a clear one makes your submission easier to process.

Keep the original, full-quality file. If the organisation later asks for a clearer copy, or a different portal accepts larger files, you will want to produce a new version from the original rather than from a compressed copy. Frequently asked questions

Why does my PDF get bigger when I try to compress it?

Text-based PDFs are already compact. A compressor that turns pages into images can produce a larger file. PDFNest detects this and gives you back the original instead.

Is 1MB the same as 1,000KB?

Not always. Some systems count 1MB as 1,000KB and others as 1,024KB. Aiming for about 950KB passes either way.

Can I get a 20-page scan under 1MB?

Often, yes, with stronger compression, but small print may soften. Check readability, and remove or split pages if the strongest level is still too large or unclear.
