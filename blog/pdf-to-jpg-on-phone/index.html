How to Convert a PDF to JPG on iPhone or Android

PBy the PDFNest Team·November 2, 2026·5 min read

Some places only take images: a social post, a chat app that previews pictures but not documents, a form that asks for a JPG. When the thing you have is a PDF on your phone, converting its pages to JPG takes a minute in the browser, with nothing to install. The step people stumble over is the last one — finding the images afterwards and getting them into the photo library.

Save the PDF where your browser can reach it

A PDF that arrived in an email or chat may only exist inside that app. Save it first: on iPhone and iPad, open the PDF, tap share and choose Save to Files; on Android, download the attachment so it appears in your Downloads folder. The browser’s file picker can then find it.

Decide which pages you actually need. Converting a 30-page document produces 30 images, and on a phone that is a lot to sort through when you only wanted the first page. If you need just one or two pages, extracting them first with Split PDF keeps the result tidy.

If the PDF is password-protected, it must be opened with the password before any page can be turned into an image. For a document you own, remove the password with Unlock PDF and convert the unlocked copy.

Convert the pages in your phone’s browser

Open PDF to JPG in Safari or Chrome, tap to choose a file and pick your PDF from Files or Downloads. Choose JPG as the format, pick a resolution, and convert. Every page of the document becomes its own image, shown as a preview you can check before downloading.

The conversion happens in the browser on your phone. The PDF is never uploaded, which matters for the things people most often convert on a phone: tickets, statements, letters with an address on them and ID documents. Keep the tab in the foreground while it works — phones pause background tabs, which can stall a long document.

JPG is the right choice for most uses because it keeps files small and every app accepts it. If the pages are mostly sharp text or line drawings and you need crisp edges, PNG is lossless and avoids the faint halo JPG can leave around small type, at the cost of larger files.

Choose a resolution that suits a phone

The resolution setting decides how many pixels each page gets. Higher is sharper when zoomed, but every step up makes each image larger and the conversion slower — and on a phone, memory runs out sooner than on a computer.

SettingGood forOn a phone
Standard — 144 DPIChat apps, social posts, quick previewsFastest, smallest files
High — 216 DPIReading text comfortably, most formsThe sensible default
Maximum — 288 DPIPrinting, zooming into fine detailSlow and memory-hungry on long PDFs

For a long document, start at Standard or High. If the conversion stalls or the tab reloads, the phone has run out of memory — try a lower setting, or split the PDF and convert it in parts. A short document at Maximum is fine; a 50-page one is better done on a computer.

Download one page or every page

For a single page, download just that image from the preview list. This is the quickest route when you only need the cover, a ticket or one page of a statement, and it avoids handling a pile of files.

For every page, use Download all as ZIP. You get one archive containing an image per page, numbered in order, which is far easier on a phone than tapping dozens of separate downloads. Both iPhone and Android can open a ZIP in their file apps — the guide to opening ZIP files covers the steps.

Note where the downloads went. On iPhone, Safari saves to the Downloads folder in the Files app; on Android, Chrome saves to the Downloads folder. People often conclude the conversion failed when the images are simply waiting there.

Get the images into your photo library

Browser downloads do not go into the photo library automatically. On iPhone, open the image in the Files app, tap share and choose Save Image; for several at once, select them in Files and share them together. Once in Photos, they can be posted or attached like any picture.

On Android, many gallery apps already show the Downloads folder as an album. If yours does not, open the image from the Files app and use share or the menu to add it to your gallery, or move the images into your Pictures folder.

If you only need the image to send in a chat, you may not need the photo library at all. Most messaging apps can attach a file directly from Files or Downloads, which saves a step and keeps your camera roll free of document pages.

Check the images before you share them

Open each image and zoom in on the smallest text. If it is soft, convert again at a higher resolution, or choose PNG for text-heavy pages. Check that the page count matches — a 12-page PDF should give 12 images, numbered in the right order.

Look at what the images contain before posting them. A page image carries everything on the page, including addresses, reference numbers and signatures, and unlike a PDF it cannot be password-protected. Crop away anything private with Crop Image before sharing it publicly.

If the destination has a size limit, reduce the images with Compress Image. For the reverse trip — combining photos back into a single PDF — the JPG to PDF guide covers that on a phone too.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an app to convert a PDF to JPG on a phone?

No. A browser-based converter works in Safari or Chrome, turns each page into an image on the phone itself and never uploads the PDF.

Which resolution should I choose on a phone?

Standard or High suits sharing and reading on screens. Maximum is only worth it for printing or zooming into fine detail, and it uses much more memory on a long document.

Why can't I find the images after downloading?

Browser downloads go to the Files app on iPhone and the Downloads folder on Android, not straight into your photo library. Save them to Photos from there.
