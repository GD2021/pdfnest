How to Split a PDF on a Mac

PBy the PDFNest Team·November 10, 2026·5 min read

Preview, the PDF app built into every Mac, can split a document — but not with a button labelled “Split”, which is why so many people go looking for other software. The method is hidden in its sidebar, and it has one trap that can change your original file. This guide covers Preview’s routes, a browser route that handles long documents faster, and how to pick between them.

Decide what kind of split you need

“Splitting” covers a few different jobs. Sometimes you need one section out of a long file — pages 12 to 18 of a contract. Sometimes you need every page as its own file, for example a batch of scanned invoices that arrived as one PDF. And sometimes you need several separate sections, each as its own document.

Before starting, note the page numbers as your viewer counts them — the position in the file, not the number printed in the corner. A report with a cover and contents may print “1” on what is really its third page.

Drag pages out of Preview

Open the PDF in Preview and show the thumbnail sidebar from the View menu. Click the thumbnail of the page you want, or Command-click to select several, then drag the selection out of the sidebar onto the desktop or into a Finder window. Preview creates a new PDF containing just those pages.

This is the safest Preview method because the original document is never edited — you are copying pages out, not removing them. The pages are copied as they are, so text stays selectable and images keep their resolution.

Repeat the drag for each section you need. The new files get generic names based on the original, so rename each one straight away; three files called “report (dragged).pdf” and similar are easy to mix up and hard to send to the right person.

Keep a range by working on a duplicate

For a long continuous range, selecting dozens of thumbnails is fiddly. The alternative is to remove everything you do not want and keep the rest — but Preview saves changes into the open file automatically. Delete pages from the original and the full document is gone unless you have another copy.

So duplicate first: choose Duplicate from the File menu and work in the copy. Select the unwanted pages in the sidebar, press Delete, then save the duplicate under a name that describes the section. The original stays complete.

If you have already changed a file by mistake, Preview may be able to bring back an earlier state through Revert To in the File menu, which lists saved versions. Check that before assuming the full document is lost — but working on a duplicate means you never have to.

Split in your browser for speed and long files

Open Split PDF in Safari or Chrome and drop the PDF onto the page. Choose Extract pages and type the pages you want — for example 12-18, or 1-3, 7, 10-12 — to get them in one new file. Pages come out in the order you type them, so 5, 1-3 puts page 5 first.

Choose Split every page instead to turn a 40-page document into 40 single-page PDFs in one step, downloaded together as a ZIP. For a batch of scanned invoices or certificates that arrived as one file, that is far quicker than forty drags in Preview.

The split runs in your browser on your Mac. The document is never uploaded and the original is never modified. Pages are copied exactly, so the output matches the source. The guide to extracting pages covers page ranges in more detail.

Avoid the print-to-PDF shortcut

A common suggestion is to open the PDF, choose Print, set a page range and then save the result as a PDF from the print dialog. It does produce a file with just those pages, but it re-prints them rather than copying them.

Re-printing can drop clickable links, bookmarks and form fields, and can change how some pages look. For a plain scan you may not notice; for a contract with links, an interactive form or a document with bookmarks, you lose things you might need.

Dragging pages out of Preview or extracting them in a browser tool copies the original pages instead, so there is no reason to accept that loss. Keep the print route for when you genuinely want a printed-style copy.

Check and name the new files

Open each new file and check the page count, the first page and the last page. Those three checks catch the common slips: using a printed page number instead of the position in the file, an off-by-one at the end of a range, or a page dragged into the wrong file.

Give every file a name that says what it contains — contract-schedule-b.pdf rather than a copy of the original name. If you split a document into several sections, numbering them keeps them in order in Finder and in email attachment lists.

Keep the original until the split files have done their job. If you need to put sections back together later, the guide to merging PDFs on a Mac covers the reverse trip.

Frequently asked questions

Can Preview split a PDF into separate files?

Yes, page by page or a selection at a time: drag thumbnails from Preview’s sidebar to the desktop and each drag becomes a new PDF. Splitting a long document into every page is quicker in a browser tool.

Does splitting a PDF reduce its quality?

No, when the pages are copied rather than printed. Dragging thumbnails in Preview and extracting pages in PDFNest both keep text selectable and images at full resolution.

Why did my original PDF change after splitting in Preview?

Preview saves edits automatically. Deleting pages from the open document to keep a section changes the original file. Work on a duplicate, or drag pages out instead of deleting them.
